How Huda chose its AI model

AAZK Solutions LLC  ·  Updated 8 October 2026  ·  Current workflow and historical model evaluation

Huda now shows references for a question instead of generating a religious answer. Its small offline search model finds related passages in downloaded source packs. Your question is not sent to an AI service.

Hadith, tafsir, and surah background summaries are prepared and checked in advance on a Mac, then included with the app. Huda does not regenerate them on your phone. The former 1.17 GB Qwen3.5-2B phone download is no longer needed for Ask or summaries.

AI summaries can still miss details. Read the full original sources, especially where reports differ, and confirm religious rulings with a qualified scholar.

What Huda does now

Ask a question. Huda searches downloaded Qur'an translations, Al-Mukhtasar, Ibn Kathir commentary, and Hadith collections. It lists related references with source excerpts and links to the full text. It does not write an answer or decide how contrasting reports should be reconciled.

Summaries. Huda displays a prepared summary only when that exact source text has a checked entry. Otherwise it offers the original source. Short passages remain verbatim. A Mac generation run uses Qwen3.5-2B IQ4_XS and source checks; entries that fail do not ship. The full text is always available.

Historical evaluation of the former on-device answer model

How we tested

  • We ran the same code that ships in the app, on a Mac, with llama.cpp. That is the engine the app uses on your phone.
  • The model's randomness was turned off, so the same question always gets the same answer. Running the tests again therefore proves nothing new. To get more evidence, we added more questions.
  • Final test set:
    • 23 questions the passages can answer.
    • 11 questions Huda must decline: off-topic, a false premise, passages that don't match, and an attempt to override the instructions.
    • 33 claims to check: 15 true and 18 false.
    • 6 summaries with a planted false sentence.
  • Answers were graded automatically, and we also read them ourselves. An answer is wrong if it states something the passages don't support, or answers a question it should have declined. A safe miss means it declined a question the passages could have answered.
  • We measured speed with the Mac's graphics chip and again with the processor alone. The second is closer to Android phones where the AI runs on the processor. Mac speeds are only for comparing models with each other. They are not phone speeds.

Why we chose IQ4_XS

  • In the final run it was as safe on answers as Q4_K_M: no wrong answers, and every refusal correct.
  • With the processor alone it was about twice as fast: 4.5 seconds per question against 9.9. Many Android phones run the AI this way.
  • Its summaries passed 6 of 6 in every run. Q4_K_M passed only 4 of 6 in two of its three earlier runs.
  • It is a 110 MB smaller download.

What we gave up. In the final run, Q4_K_M caught all 18 false claims. IQ4_XS let two through:

  • "Emigrating for worldly benefit earns the same reward as emigrating for Allah."
  • "Allah takes knowledge away by erasing it from the hearts of people."

Q4_K_M also answered two more questions. The claim check is not the only safeguard: every answer must cite the passages, and Huda shows them with it. Even so, this is a real difference, and it is the first thing we will measure again.

Mistakes we saw with the chosen model

  • Incomplete answer. Asked about drinking water while sitting, it said the Prophet ﷺ drank both standing and sitting. It left out the narration in which he disapproved of drinking while standing.
  • True claims rejected. It rejected two true claims. One was that a narration reports the Prophet ﷺ disapproved of drinking while standing. The other was that a man who fears he cannot be just between wives should marry only one.
  • A wrong name in a summary. In the processor-only run, its summary of Surah Al-Fath's background called it "Surah Al-Kahf", and the summary check did not catch it. Summaries can contain mistakes like this, so read the original when it matters.
  • Many questions declined. It gave no answer to 13 of the 23 questions the passages could answer. We would rather it decline than guess, but it means Ask often shows you only the passages.

What these tests cannot tell you

  • The test set is small: 34 questions, 33 claims and 6 summaries, all written by us. It is not an independent or scholarly review.
  • The tests ran on a Mac, not on phones. Phones are slower, and results on a phone may differ slightly.
  • Answers were graded by automatic checks and by our own reading. We can miss things.
  • Passing these tests does not mean an answer is correct in matters of religion. Treat Ask as a way to find passages to read, not as a ruling. Ask a qualified scholar.
